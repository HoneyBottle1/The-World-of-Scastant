# Important Information

**One sentence definition of the character:** The focus here is a sentence where the character is the hero of _their_ story and not how they relate to _the_ story. Example: Harry Potter is an orphan boy who learns he is a wizard and attends Hogwarts. Severus Snape is a man who must live with the fact that the love of his life is dead and he has to teach her child with another man. (You get the picture.)

**Affiliations pre-story:** I am writing a complex narrative because really I cannot bear to make anything simple for myself, so it helps to know what groups or people my characters (know/belong to/owe money to) before the story begins and how.

**The character's motivations:** Both concrete and not concrete. Focus on things they want to achieve, things they're afraid of losing will come later.

**The character's goal in the story:** Often a character does not start out knowing what their goal is, so I think of it as their Ultimate Goal.

**The character's conflict:** Generally, how do one or more of their high level goals conflict.

**How do your characters motivations change?** What do they learn, how do they grow, and how do they change? Do their motivations change, and to what?

**Tags:** Keywords used to physically describe a character that you refrain from using elsewhere to create an unconscious connection to your character with your reader. I use these in lieu of giving my reader a full physical description of every single one of my characters. Example: Harry Potter's lightning scar.

**Traits:** Conceptual ideas about how your character approaches the world and/or certain talismans they have. Examples: Dry sense of humor, love of art (Really just any little thing that is ancillary to the story and gives the character a slight bit of uniqueness.) Example: One of my favorite DnD characters had a book that could not be written in with any ink, magical or no. He was sort of crazy and believed that all of the secrets of the universe were in it.

**What is this character's plot or subplot in the story?** Simplicity argues that each arc, including the main story arc, should have a character assigned to it. Complexity argues that each major character should have their own arc. (Note: It is here where you should realize whether or not your character has a purpose or not being in your story. General rule of thumb, if the subplot doesn't create conflict, cut it.)

(Thusly, I am sure your character, like all of us shut-in writing hermits, leads a terribly vibrant, rich life and has plenty of friends, acquaintances, nemeses (is that the proper pluralization?). I think the character sheet should figure out why they are there in the story though.) _How does this characters subplot connect to the main story arc?_ (This is pretty self explanatory for your main character, but flesh it out for those it isn't)

**What does your character not want others knowing?** What deep dark secrets do they have or what are they unsure about. In other words, what's their greatest weakness?

**How does the character see himself?** (Every villain is likely not the villain of their own story. Make your character describe (him/her/it/owlkin)self to you) (This should be reflected in your one sentence at the top, but this should be more fleshed out, like if your character were asked awkward interview questions applying for a job position they really wanted.) Note: I am doing this step to create empathy in the character for myself, so I can write (him/her/it/owlkin) empathetically later. Philosophy/method/style: Every character should see themselves as at least neutral, if a character sees themselves as evil, then you probably need to get to know them better.

**What have they lost/never known?** In other words, what experiences have the had that would be different from average and how would it color their worldview. EX: I have a character that was orphaned at a young age (I know it's a trope, but sadly it mirrors my own experience loosely so I get a pass I think.) It makes him value the people that stick around much more, to the point of holding onto some toxic relationships.
